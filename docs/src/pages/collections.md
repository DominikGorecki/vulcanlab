# Collections

## Overview

Collections are a powerful feature in VulcanLab that allow you to organize and curate research materials into focused, topic-specific repositories. Think of a collection as a digital research folder where you gather excerpts, previous research results, and research questions related to a specific subject area.

Collections serve as the foundation for VulcanLab's advanced [Deep Research](./deep-research-collections-guide.md) feature, which can automatically synthesize all your collected materials into comprehensive, academic-quality reports.

---

## What is a Collection?

A **collection** is a curated set of research materials organized around a specific topic or research question. Collections help you:

- **Organize** disparate research materials in one place
- **Contextualize** your research with descriptions and tags
- **Synthesize** information through Deep Research
- **Track** what you've read and what matters most
- **Collaborate** (future feature) by sharing collections with others

### Collection Metadata

Each collection has:

- **Name** (required): A descriptive title for your research area
- **Description** (optional): Details about the collection's scope and purpose
- **Tags** (optional): Comma-separated keywords for organization and filtering
- **Timestamps**: Automatic tracking of creation and modification dates
- **Item Count**: Number of items in the collection

---

## What Can You Put in a Collection?

Collections can contain three types of items:

### 1. Excerpts

**Excerpts** are highlighted passages or quotes from your library—academic papers, books, articles, or other documents you've indexed in VulcanLab.

**What they contain:**
- The exact text passage from your source material
- Source metadata (title, authors, year, work ID)
- Heading breadcrumbs showing where the excerpt appears in the document
- Line number references for precise location

**How to add them:**
- While viewing search results or browsing documents
- Click "Add to Collection" on any passage
- Select or create a collection

**Example use cases:**
- Key quotes supporting your thesis
- Definitions of important concepts
- Data points or statistics
- Methodology descriptions
- Contrasting viewpoints

### 2. Research Results

**Research Results** are complete answers generated by VulcanLab's RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) system in response to research questions you've asked.

**What they contain:**
- The original research question (query text)
- The full generated response with citations
- Metadata about which sources were used
- Preview of the response content

**How to add them:**
- From previous RAG query results
- Click "Add to Collection" on any research result
- Useful for building on prior work

**Example use cases:**
- Answers to related sub-questions
- Background research completed earlier
- Comparative analyses you want to reference
- Literature reviews on adjacent topics
- Synthesized explanations to build upon

### 3. Research Queries

**Research Queries** are research questions or prompts you've formulated that relate to your collection's topic.

**What they contain:**
- The query text (research question)
- Query metadata and ID

**How to add them:**
- Save interesting questions as you think of them
- Add queries from your RAG history
- Plan future research directions

**Example use cases:**
- Questions to explore later
- Sub-questions that break down complex topics
- Variations of your main research question
- Related areas you want to investigate

## Creating and Managing Collections

### Creating a New Collection

1. **From the Collections Page:**
   - Navigate to the Collections section
   - Click "Create New Collection"
   - Enter a name (required, max 255 characters)
   - Add a description (optional but recommended)
   - Add tags (optional, comma-separated)
   - Click "Create"

2. **From the Add to Collection Modal:**
   - While adding an item from search or RAG results
   - Click "Create New Collection" in the modal
   - Fill in details and add the item in one step

### Viewing Your Collections

**Collections List View:**

The main collections page shows all your collections in a table with:
- **Name** with description tooltip on hover
- **Tags** for quick categorization
- **Item Count** showing how many items are in each
- **Created Date** and **Last Modified** dates
- **Actions** (delete, navigate to detail)

You can:
- Sort by any column
- Search by name or description
- Filter by tags
- Click any row to view collection details

### Collection Detail View

When you open a collection, you see:

**Header Section:**
- Collection name and metadata
- Item count and last modified date
- Tags (editable)
- Description (click to edit inline)

**In-Progress Research:**
- Active manual or automated research sessions
- Resume buttons for paused research
- Progress indicators

**Deep Research Button:**
- Available when you have 5 or more items
- Launches comprehensive research synthesis
- See [Deep Research Guide](./deep-research-collections-guide.md) for details

**Collection Items Table:**
Shows all items in your collection with:
- **Checkbox**: Select items for bulk operations
- **Order**: Numeric field you can edit to reorder items
- **Type Badge**: Visual indicator (Excerpt, Research Result, Research Query)
- **Resource**: Link to the original source with expand button
- **Note**: Personal notes you can add inline
- **Metadata Card**: Expandable detailed information
- **Actions**: Delete individual items

## Working with Collection Items

### Adding Items

**From Search Results:**
1. Perform a search in VulcanLab
2. Find a relevant passage or result
3. Click "Add to Collection"
4. Choose an existing collection or create a new one
5. Optionally add a note
6. Click "Add"

**From RAG Results:**
1. Complete a research query
2. Review the generated results
3. Click "Add to Collection" on useful results
4. Select or create a collection
5. Add optional notes

### Organizing Items

**Reordering:**
- Click on any item's order field
- Enter a new number (supports decimals for precise ordering)
- Press Enter to save
- Items are sorted by order value

**Adding Notes:**
- Click on the note field for any item
- Type your thoughts, context, or reminders
- Notes auto-save when you click away
- Use notes to:
  - Record why this item matters
  - Add context or interpretation
  - Note connections to other items
  - Flag items for follow-up

**Viewing Metadata:**
- Click the expand button on any item
- See enriched metadata specific to the item type:
  - **Excerpts**: Full source information, breadcrumbs, preview
  - **Research Results**: Original query, response preview
  - **Research Queries**: Full query text

### Bulk Operations

**Bulk Delete:**
1. Select multiple items using checkboxes
2. Click "Delete Selected"
3. Confirm the action
4. Selected items are permanently removed

## Collection Strategies and Best Practices

### Focused vs. Broad Collections

**Focused Collections** (Recommended):
- Single research question or narrow topic
- 10-30 highly relevant items
- Clear, specific scope
- Better for Deep Research synthesis
- Example: "Machine Learning Bias in Healthcare"

**Broad Collections**:
- Multiple related sub-topics
- 30-100+ items
- General area of interest
- Good for exploratory research
- Example: "Artificial Intelligence Overview"

**Recommendation**: Start focused. You can always create multiple related collections rather than one massive collection.

### Curation Tips

1. **Quality over Quantity:**
   - Don't add everything—be selective
   - Each item should contribute something unique
   - Remove items that become irrelevant

2. **Use Descriptive Names:**
   - "Climate Policy 2015-2025" > "Climate Stuff"
   - "Neural Network Interpretability Methods" > "ML Research"
   - Names should indicate scope clearly

3. **Write Good Descriptions:**
   - Explain the collection's purpose
   - Note what's included and excluded
   - Helps with future recall and Deep Research planning

4. **Tag Thoughtfully:**
   - Use consistent tag vocabulary
   - Include: domain, methodology, time period, etc.
   - Examples: "climate-science", "meta-analysis", "2020s", "policy"

5. **Add Meaningful Notes:**
   - Why did you include this item?
   - How does it relate to others?
   - What's the key takeaway?
   - Notes improve context for Deep Research

6. **Include Diverse Sources:**
   - Mix of supporting and contrasting views
   - Different methodologies or approaches
   - Various publication dates
   - Multiple authors or institutions
   - Diversity improves research quality

7. **Maintain and Prune:**
   - Periodically review collections
   - Remove outdated or less relevant items
   - Add new items as you discover them
   - Keep collections living and current

### When to Create Multiple Collections

Consider splitting into separate collections when:
- You have distinct sub-questions requiring different sources
- One topic has 50+ items (hard to navigate)
- Materials serve different purposes (literature review vs. methodology)
- Time periods are distinct (historical vs. contemporary)
- You want different Deep Research reports for different angles

## Deep Research Integration

Collections are the foundation for VulcanLab's **Deep Research** feature, which automatically synthesizes your collected materials into comprehensive research reports.

### Requirements

To use Deep Research, you need:
- At least **5 items** in your collection
- A mix of item types is ideal (excerpts + research results work best)
- Related materials on a cohesive topic

### How It Works

Deep Research analyzes your entire collection and:
1. Understands your research topic from collection name, description, and content
2. Generates focused sub-questions that break down your topic
3. Finds existing research results to reuse (cost and time savings)
4. Selects relevant excerpts for each sub-question
5. Generates detailed, cited answers for each question
6. Evaluates quality (citation coverage, source diversity, coherence)
7. Synthesizes everything into a polished final report

### Two Modes

**Manual Mode:**
- Step-by-step control
- Review and approve at each stage
- Edit research plan and questions
- Choose what to reuse
- Full transparency

**Automated Mode:**
- One-click operation
- Handles everything automatically
- Progress indicators
- Notification when complete
- Great for quick insights

For complete details, see the [Deep Research Collections Guide](./deep-research-collections-guide.md).

## Technical Details

### Data Model

Collections are stored with:
- Unique ID (auto-generated)
- User ownership (linked to your account)
- Metadata (name, description, tags)
- Timestamps (created, updated)
- Relationships to items and research sessions

Collection items are stored with:
- Unique ID
- Collection ID (foreign key)
- Item type (enum: excerpt, research_result, research_query)
- Link (formatted path to original resource)
- Note (optional text)
- Order (decimal number for flexible sorting)
- Timestamps (added, modified)

### Link Formats

Items use specific link formats to reference original resources:

**Excerpts:**
```
/search/result/{work_id}/{start_line}/{end_line}
```
Example: `/search/result/123/45/67`

**Research Results:**
```
/rag/{query_id}/results/{result_id}
```
Example: `/rag/456/results/789`

**Research Queries:**
```
/rag/{query_id}
```
Example: `/rag/456`

### API Endpoints

Collections are accessible via REST API:

| Method | Endpoint | Purpose |
|--------|----------|---------|
| GET | `/api/v1/collections/` | List all collections (paginated) |
| POST | `/api/v1/collections/` | Create new collection |
| GET | `/api/v1/collections/{id}` | Get collection details |
| PATCH | `/api/v1/collections/{id}` | Update collection metadata |
| DELETE | `/api/v1/collections/{id}` | Delete collection |
| POST | `/api/v1/collections/{id}/items` | Add item to collection |
| PATCH | `/api/v1/collections/{id}/items/{item_id}` | Update item |
| DELETE | `/api/v1/collections/{id}/items/{item_id}` | Delete item |
| DELETE | `/api/v1/collections/{id}/items` | Bulk delete items |
| GET | `/api/v1/collections/{id}/items/{item_id}/metadata` | Get enriched metadata |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How many items should a collection have?**

A: There's no strict limit, but 10-30 items is ideal for focused research. You need at least 5 items to use Deep Research.

**Q: Can I move items between collections?**

A: Currently, you'd need to add the item to the new collection and remove it from the old one. A direct move feature may be added in the future.

**Q: What happens if I delete a source document?**

A: The collection item remains, but the link may become invalid. Excerpts store a preview, so you'll still have the text even if the source is removed.

**Q: Can I export a collection?**

A: You can export Deep Research reports generated from collections. Direct collection export may be added in the future.

**Q: How are collections different from bookmarks or folders?**

A: Collections are intelligent—they store not just links, but rich metadata and context. They integrate with Deep Research for automatic synthesis. They're research tools, not just organizational tools.

**Q: Can I share collections with others?**

A: Not currently, but collection sharing is planned for a future release.

**Q: Do collections have a size limit?**

A: No hard limit, but very large collections (100+ items) may be slower to process in Deep Research.

**Q: What if I have duplicate items?**

A: Deep Research deduplicates content automatically, so duplicates won't appear multiple times in reports.
